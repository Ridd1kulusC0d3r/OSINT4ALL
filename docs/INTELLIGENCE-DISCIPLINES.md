# Intelligence Disciplines, Functions & OSINT Specializations
# Disciplinas, Funções de Inteligência e Especializações OSINT

> **Working taxonomy for repository navigation.** Different governments, services, academic communities and private-sector teams use overlapping or incompatible definitions. OSINT4ALL therefore separates collection disciplines, analytical functions, levels of intelligence and practitioner specializations.

## 1. Core collection disciplines

| Discipline | Meaning | Typical open-source overlap |
|---|---|---|
| **OSINT** | Open Source Intelligence | Public records, web, media, datasets, research |
| **HUMINT** | Human Intelligence | Public interviews, witness material and human-source context; clandestine/private collection is outside this repo |
| **SIGINT** | Signals Intelligence | Public spectrum research, technical publications and openly available telemetry |
| **COMINT** | Communications Intelligence | Public communications research and openly published communications datasets |
| **ELINT** | Electronic Intelligence | Radar/emitter research using public technical and geospatial sources |
| **FISINT** | Foreign Instrumentation Signals Intelligence | Public research on telemetry/instrumentation signatures |
| **GEOINT** | Geospatial Intelligence | Maps, satellite imagery, terrain, geolocation and change detection |
| **IMINT** | Imagery Intelligence | Image/video interpretation, metadata and visual verification |
| **MASINT** | Measurement and Signature Intelligence | Public sensor datasets, spectral/physical signatures and scientific measurements |

### Important overlap

SIGINT is commonly treated as an umbrella that can include COMINT, ELINT and FISINT. IMINT is historically associated with imagery analysis, while GEOINT is broader and integrates imagery with geospatial information.

The exact boundaries depend on the institution.

## 2. Intelligence functions and analytical forms

These are not always "collection INTs", but analysts encounter them constantly.

| Function / form | Focus |
|---|---|
| **All-Source Intelligence** | Fusion of multiple intelligence disciplines and sources |
| **Counterintelligence (CI)** | Identifying and countering espionage, subversion and hostile intelligence activity |
| **Indications & Warning (I&W)** | Detecting signals that may indicate an emerging event or threat |
| **Current Intelligence** | Time-sensitive reporting on ongoing events |
| **Estimative Intelligence** | Structured assessment of future developments and uncertainty |
| **Strategic Intelligence** | Long-horizon policy, geopolitical, economic or organizational context |
| **Operational Intelligence** | Intelligence supporting campaigns, operations or sustained activity |
| **Tactical Intelligence** | Immediate, specific intelligence supporting short-term decisions |
| **Scientific & Technical Intelligence (S&TI)** | Scientific, engineering and technical capability analysis |
| **Economic Intelligence** | Macroeconomic, sector, trade, sanctions and financial context |
| **Political Intelligence** | Institutions, parties, policy processes, leadership and political developments |
| **Military Intelligence** | Armed forces, capabilities, doctrine, logistics and order of battle |
| **Law-Enforcement / Criminal Intelligence** | Crime patterns, entities, networks and investigative support |
| **Security Intelligence** | Threats to public, institutional or organizational security |
| **Competitive / Market Intelligence** | Companies, markets, competitors, technology and commercial ecosystems |
| **Business Intelligence** | Internal/market data analysis for organizational decision-making; not synonymous with intelligence collection |
| **Risk Intelligence** | Structured analysis of threats, vulnerabilities, exposure and uncertainty |

## 4. Physical-world and situational specializations

- **Satellite / Space OSINT** — TLEs, orbital tracking, Earth observation and space situational awareness.
- **Aviation Intelligence** — aircraft registries, ADS-B, airports, routes and aviation infrastructure.
- **Maritime Intelligence** — AIS, ship registries, ports, fishing and maritime activity.
- **Environmental Intelligence** — deforestation, fires, land use, pollution, wildlife and environmental crime.
- **Climate / Weather Intelligence** — meteorological and climate datasets used for verification and context.
- **Conflict OSINT** — geolocation, chronology, verification and situational awareness in armed conflict.
- **CBRNE Intelligence** — chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear and explosive threat research.
- **Nuclear OSINT** — public-source analysis of nuclear facilities, policy, proliferation, imagery and monitoring.
- **Critical Infrastructure / OT Intelligence** — defensive visibility into ICS/SCADA/OT ecosystems and public exposure.
- **Urban Intelligence** — cities, infrastructure, mobility, planning, utilities and municipal systems.
- **Border / Migration Intelligence** — lawful research into border systems, migration flows and policy.
- **Humanitarian Intelligence** — public-source support to humanitarian situational awareness and needs assessment.
- **Disaster Intelligence** — earthquakes, fires, floods, storms, damage assessment and response context.

## 5. Investigative specializations

- **People OSINT** — names, aliases, usernames, email, phone, public records and relationship mapping.
- **SOCMINT** — social profiles, posts, networks, communities, narratives and platform-specific research.
- **Corporate OSINT** — legal entities, ownership, directors, procurement, litigation and corporate networks.
- **Fraud / KYC / AML OSINT** — entity verification, sanctions, adverse media and suspicious relationship discovery.
- **Geolocation & Chronolocation** — determining where and when media/events occurred.
- **Image / Video Verification** — authenticity, provenance, reverse search, frame analysis and metadata.
- **Cyber Infrastructure OSINT** — domains, IPs, certificates, exposed services and internet infrastructure.
- **Code / Repository Intelligence** — public source code, commits, dependency context and exposed artifacts.
- **Breach / Exposure Intelligence** — public reporting and lawful exposure datasets; credential abuse is out of scope.
- **Disinformation / Narrative Intelligence** — information operations, narratives, amplification and source tracing.
- **Elections / Civic OSINT** — official electoral data, campaign finance, public statements and civic records.
- **Legal / Court OSINT** — court decisions, dockets, statutes, regulatory and enforcement records.
- **Academic / Scientific OSINT** — papers, patents, grants, institutions, datasets and researchers.
- **Archive Intelligence** — web archives, historical documents and change-over-time analysis.
- **Real-Estate / Land Intelligence** — land registries, cadastre, property and zoning data.
- **Procurement Intelligence** — tenders, contracts, suppliers and public expenditure.
- **Media Intelligence** — news ecosystems, source provenance and narrative tracking.
- **Patent / Innovation Intelligence** — patents, inventors, assignees and technology trends.
- **Academic Network Intelligence** — institutions, labs, grants, researchers and publication networks.
- **Transportation Intelligence** — aviation, maritime, rail, road and logistics networks.
- **Telecommunications Intelligence** — public telecom infrastructure, numbering, routing and operator context.
- **Domain / DNS Intelligence** — DNS, certificates, domain ownership/history and related infrastructure.
- **Geopolitical Intelligence** — state behavior, institutions, conflict, diplomacy and international systems.

## 6. Intelligence by decision horizon

Another useful classification is by **when the intelligence is meant to support a decision**:

| Level | Typical horizon | Example output |
|---|---|---|
| **Strategic** | Months to years | Geopolitical, sector or threat landscape assessment |
| **Operational** | Days to months | Campaign, actor or organizational activity assessment |
| **Tactical** | Minutes to days | Immediate indicator, event or entity-focused intelligence |

CTI often uses a similar strategic / operational / tactical split, though organizations define the boundaries differently.

## 7. Intelligence-cycle mapping

A resource can support one or more intelligence-cycle stages:

1. **Direction / Planning**
2. **Collection**
3. **Processing / Exploitation**
4. **Analysis / Production**
5. **Dissemination**
6. **Feedback / Evaluation**

OSINT4ALL will eventually tag resources by cycle stage so users can search by workflow rather than tool name.

## 8. Cross-domain examples

**Sentinel / Copernicus** can map to GEOINT, IMINT, Environmental Intelligence, Conflict OSINT and Satellite OSINT.

**MISP** can map to CTI, CYBINT, Threat Intelligence and IoC sharing.

**Maltego** can support People OSINT, Corporate OSINT, CTI, Identity Intelligence and relationship analysis.

**OpenCorporates** can support Corporate OSINT, FININT, sanctions research, supply-chain intelligence and identity correlation.

The model is intentionally many-to-many. Real investigations do not respect folder boundaries.

## 9. Repository tagging model

Each resource should eventually receive:

- collection discipline(s);
- analytical domain(s);
- intelligence level(s);
- jurisdiction(s);
- use case(s);
- intelligence-cycle stage(s);
- source type;
- validation status.

Example:

    name: Example Resource
    disciplines: [OSINT, GEOINT]
    domains: [environmental-intelligence]
    levels: [strategic, operational]
    cycle: [collection, processing, analysis]
    jurisdictions: [GLOBAL, BR]
    status: needs-review

## 11. Taxonomy rule

When a new acronym appears, do not create a new top-level category merely because someone on the internet added "INT" to a noun.

First determine whether it is:

- a recognized collection discipline;
- an analytical function;
- an organizational label;
- an application domain;
- or simply a useful practitioner shorthand.

OSINT4ALL can track all five, but it should label them honestly.
